A Glimpse into Washougal’s History

Washougal’s history dates back to the Chinook and Klickitat peoples, with the name “Washougal” derived from a Chinook term meaning “rushing water.” Founded in 1832 by fur traders and incorporated in 1908, the city grew as a lumber and trade hub, leveraging its strategic location near the Columbia River Gorge. The Washougal River and historic downtown, with landmarks like the Two Rivers Heritage Museum, reflect its pioneer roots. By the 20th century, Washougal became part of Clark County’s growth, with industries like Pendleton Woolen Mills shaping its economy.

Today, Washougal balances its heritage with modern development, offering parks like Steamboat Landing and events like the Washougal Art Festival. The Educational Landscape of Washougal

Washougal is served by the Washougal School District (WSD), enrolling 2,978 students across nine public schools: five elementary schools, two middle schools, one high school, and one alternative school, as of the 2024-2025 school year. WSD ranks in the top 50% of Washington districts, with 38% math proficiency and 55% reading proficiency, compared to state averages of 40% and 53%. Washougal High School, with 990 students, ranks #122 in Washington, offering Advanced Placement (AP) courses, a Project Lead The Way curriculum, and a Gifted & Talented program. Its 41% AP participation rate, 36% math proficiency, and 71% reading proficiency highlight strengths, though math scores lag behind the state’s 40%. The school’s 85-89% graduation rate exceeds the state’s 84%. Parents praise its fine arts programs, including band and culinary arts.

Top-performing schools include Mount Pleasant School, a K-8 district with 65 students, ranking in the top 5% of Washington schools with 64% math and 75% reading proficiency. Gause Elementary (274 students) and Canyon Creek Middle School (230 students) rank in the top 50%, with Gause achieving 45-49% math and 60-64% reading proficiency. Jemtegaard Middle School (433 students) ranks in the bottom 50%, with 32% math and 52% reading proficiency, but earns high marks for its engaging teachers and community involvement. Hathaway Elementary (257 students) also ranks in the bottom 50%, with 45-49% math and 40-44% reading proficiency. WSD’s diversity is lower than the state average, with 21% minority enrollment (13% Hispanic).

Private school options in Washougal are limited, with Riverside Adventist Christian School serving 56 students in grades K-8, emphasizing faith-based education. Approximately 2% of Washougal’s K-12 students attend private schools, lower than the state’s 10% average, due to WSD’s quality. Nearby Vancouver offers additional private schools like King’s Way Christian. Homeschooling in Washougal

A Growing Option

Homeschooling is gaining popularity in Washougal, mirroring Washington’s active homeschooling community. In 2024-2025, ~7% of Washington’s K-12 students (77,000) are homeschooled, up from 6% in 2021-2022, with ~5,000 in Clark County, including Washougal. Nationally, 6.73% of students (3.7 million) are homeschooled, a 51% increase over six years. Washington’s homeschoolers score at the 65th-75th percentile on standardized tests, 10-20 points above public school peers. The COVID-19 pandemic drove a 30% surge in 2020-2021, with families citing flexibility (75%) and dissatisfaction with traditional instruction (40%). Washington requires homeschoolers to file an annual Declaration of Intent and cover 11 subjects, with optional testing.

Washougal families access resources like the Washougal Community Library’s STEM and literacy programs and co-ops in Vancouver, such as the Southwest Washington Homeschool Network. The Washougal Learning Academy (0.2 miles from key schools) offers hybrid learning options.
